01 / Project Overview

The instrument and the user workflow.

At KGI, approximately 50 students worked on MANISA, a proposed next-generation sequencing (NGS) instrument for pneumonia and antimicrobial resistance testing. I spent the first part of the project on the Hardware team and the second on Human Factors Engineering. Both roles required working with other teams to account for their requirements.

I created the instrument's 3D CAD model and internal component layout, then worked on the seven-step operator workflow and interface mockups. The designs changed as we gathered feedback from other teams.

This was a design concept, not a fabricated or clinically validated instrument.

02 / Hardware Engineering

Instrument architecture and CAD.

I built the benchtop enclosure and internal layout in CAD, working around the space needed for robotic movement, sample handling, waste disposal, and operator access.

The proposed layout included sample and reagent storage, a thermomixer, incubation and magnetic bead separation stations, a barcode scanner, and sequencing-related components.

03 / Human Factors Engineering

Operator workflow and interface design.

On the Human Factors team, I mapped the steps an operator would take from receiving a sample to exporting results. I also made interface mockups for instrument setup and sample handling, working through what users would need to see and do at each stage.

The sequence covers sample receipt, preparation, instrument setup, loading, operation, result generation, and export.

05 / Reflection

What this project taught me.

I worked on the instrument from two sides: where the components would fit and how someone would use it. Switching from hardware to human factors made it clear that those decisions couldn't be made independently. The most interesting part was working through the tradeoffs with people responsible for other parts of the system.
